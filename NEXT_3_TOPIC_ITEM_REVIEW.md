Purpose: identify whether the next three topic sets are likely to produce useful "Key tensions" reporting rather than only broad consensus.

## Review Standard

Each weekly topic should include:

- A few consensus anchors for context.
- Several boundary or policy items likely to produce spread.
- At least 2-3 items likely to reveal reportable key tensions.
- Clear wording that can be rated on a 0-100 credence slider.

## Week 2 Candidate: Pornography and the Church

Overall assessment: strong, but several items may trend toward high agreement among ministry participants. This topic should still produce useful disagreement because it includes disqualification, leadership screening, frequency of teaching, behavioral-health framing, and pastoral response to spouses.

Strong tension items:

- Q4: Persistent pornography use should ordinarily disqualify a pastor from active ministry until repentance and accountability are demonstrably established.
- Q5: Churches should ask direct but pastorally careful questions about pornography use when evaluating candidates for leadership.
- Q8: Pastors should address pornography from the pulpit more often than most churches currently do.
- Q9: Churches should treat habitual pornography use as both moral sin and a behavioral health concern.
- Q10: In my ministry context, churches are currently losing more ground than they are gaining against pornography.

Possible consensus-prone items:

- Q1: Pornography use is among the most spiritually damaging hidden sins currently affecting churches.
- Q7: Accountability software is usually helpful but insufficient by itself for addressing habitual pornography use.
- Q11: Churches should provide pastoral care for spouses harmed by a partner's persistent pornography use.
- Q12: Churches should teach young people how pornography shapes desire before they are likely to encounter it.

Suggested sharpening before fielding:

- Q1 could become: "Pornography use is more spiritually damaging to churches today than most other hidden sins."
- Q7 could become: "Churches should avoid presenting accountability software as a primary solution for habitual pornography use."
- Q11 could become: "Churches should provide structured pastoral care for spouses harmed by pornography use even when the user is repentant."
- Q12 could become: "Churches should teach young people about pornography before parents or students report that it is already a problem."

Recommendation: fieldable after a light sharpening pass. Preserve Q4, Q5, Q8, Q9, and Q10.

## Week 3 Candidate: Pastoral Authority and Accountability

Overall assessment: very strong. This topic already balances authority, safeguards, polity, external accountability, spiritual-abuse language, complaints, finances, and confidential information. It should generate meaningful spread without much revision.

Strong tension items:

- Q1: In many churches, pastors exercise less legitimate spiritual authority than Scripture assigns to church leaders.
- Q2: In many churches, pastors exercise more practical authority than their accountability structures can safely support.
- Q4: Congregational voting is an important safeguard against pastoral misuse of authority.
- Q5: Pastors should be accountable to some recognized authority outside their own local congregation.
- Q7: In my ministry context, the label spiritual abuse is sometimes applied to ordinary pastoral disagreement or correction.
- Q9: Pastoral authority should include giving moral counsel about members' major life decisions outside formal church programs.
- Q10: Churches should publish clear procedures for bringing complaints against pastors, elders, or other leaders.
- Q12: Churches should distinguish between biblical submission to leaders and unquestioning compliance with leaders.

Possible consensus-prone items:

- Q3: A plurality of qualified elders is an important safeguard against pastoral misuse of authority.
- Q11: Pastors should have meaningful accountability for how they use church finances, staff authority, and confidential information.

Suggested sharpening before fielding:

- Q3 could become: "A plurality of qualified elders is a stronger safeguard against pastoral misuse of authority than congregational voting."
- Q11 could become: "Church members should receive enough information to evaluate how pastors use church finances, staff authority, and confidential information."

Recommendation: fieldable now. Any edits should be modest because the topic already has high tension potential.

## Week 4 Candidate: Women in Church Leadership

Overall assessment: very strong. This topic is naturally tension-rich and includes scriptural permission, governing authority, deacon roles, first-order doctrine, separation, decision-making, forecasts, and leadership-gift evaluation.

Strong tension items:

- Q1: Scripture permits women to preach to mixed adult congregations in the gathered church.
- Q2: Scripture permits women to serve as elders or overseers in the local church.
- Q3: Scripture permits women to serve as deacons in the local church.
- Q4: The New Testament restricts women from exercising final governing authority over men in the local church.
- Q6: Egalitarian interpretations of the relevant biblical texts are exegetically plausible.
- Q7: A church's position on women serving as elders should be treated as a first-order doctrinal issue.
- Q8: Churches should normally separate from one another over incompatible positions on women serving as elders.
- Q10: Over the next 20 years, evangelical churches will become substantially more egalitarian in practice.

Possible consensus-prone items:

- Q5: Complementarian churches often fail to make adequate use of women's spiritual gifts.
- Q9: Even in complementarian churches, women should have meaningful involvement in major church decision-making.
- Q12: Churches should evaluate leadership gifts in women with the same seriousness they apply to leadership gifts in men.

Suggested sharpening before fielding:

- Q5 could become: "Complementarian restrictions on office often cause churches to underuse women's spiritual gifts."
- Q9 could become: "Complementarian churches should include women in major decision-making even when final elder authority is male-only."
- Q12 could become: "Churches should formally evaluate leadership gifts in women even when those women are not eligible for every leadership office."

Recommendation: fieldable now. Consider sharpening Q5, Q9, and Q12 only if the survey needs more distributional spread.
