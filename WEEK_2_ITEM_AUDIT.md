## Fielding Decision

Field Week 2 as `Pornography and the Church`.

The topic is pastorally relevant, broadly recognizable, and likely to generate interpretable spread around disqualification, leadership screening, pulpit frequency, behavioral-health framing, and whether churches are gaining or losing ground. Some items may produce strong agreement, but the topic has enough practical and ecclesial tension to justify fielding.

## Sharpened CTS-Administered Items

Default response format: 0 = strongly disagree, 50 = unsure/mixed, 100 = strongly agree.

1. Pornography use is more spiritually damaging to churches today than most other hidden sins.
   - Sharpened from the topic-bank version to invite comparison with other hidden sins and reduce consensus drift.
2. In my ministry context, pornography use among Christian men is more common than most congregations recognize.
   - Fieldable as written.
3. In my ministry context, pornography use among Christian women is more common than most congregations recognize.
   - Fieldable as written; likely useful because it tests awareness beyond the more commonly discussed male-use frame.
4. Persistent pornography use should ordinarily disqualify a pastor from active ministry until repentance and accountability are demonstrably established.
   - Fieldable as written; high tension potential around disqualification and restoration.
5. Churches should ask direct but pastorally careful questions about pornography use when evaluating candidates for leadership.
   - Fieldable as written; practical screening question.
6. Pornography use is substantially shaping sexual expectations within Christian marriages.
   - Fieldable as written; pastoral and sociological interpretive value.
7. Churches should avoid presenting accountability software as a primary solution for habitual pornography use.
   - Sharpened from the topic-bank version to test a more actionable claim about church practice.
8. Pastors should address pornography from the pulpit more often than most churches currently do.
   - Fieldable as written; likely variation by ministry context and preaching philosophy.
9. Churches should treat habitual pornography use as both moral sin and a behavioral health concern.
   - Fieldable as written; tests moral/therapeutic framing.
10. In my ministry context, churches are currently losing more ground than they are gaining against pornography.
    - Fieldable as written; useful broad ministry-context judgment.
11. Churches should provide structured pastoral care for spouses harmed by pornography use even when the user is repentant.
    - Sharpened to test structured care and the harder case where repentance is present.
12. Churches should teach young people about pornography before parents or students report that it is already a problem.
    - Sharpened to test proactive timing rather than only general youth instruction.
