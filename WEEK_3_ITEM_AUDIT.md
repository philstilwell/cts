## Fielding Decision

Field Week 3 as `Pastoral Authority and Accountability`, subject to CTS review of the ballot overlap note below.

The topic is pastorally relevant and likely to produce useful spread around spiritual authority, practical authority, elder plurality, congregational voting, external accountability, spiritual-abuse labeling, complaint procedures, and leader use of finance/staff/confidential authority. ## Sharpened CTS-Administered Items

Default response format: 0 = strongly disagree, 50 = unsure/mixed, 100 = strongly agree.

1. In many churches, pastors exercise less legitimate spiritual authority than Scripture assigns to church leaders.
   - Fieldable as written; pairs with item 2 to test under-authority and over-authority concerns.
2. In many churches, pastors exercise more practical authority than their accountability structures can safely support.
   - Fieldable as written; high relevance to governance and safeguarding.
3. A plurality of qualified elders is an important safeguard against pastoral misuse of authority.
   - Fieldable as written; likely to reveal polity differences.
4. Congregational voting is an important safeguard against pastoral misuse of authority.
   - Fieldable as written; likely to reveal polity differences distinct from elder plurality.
5. Pastors should be accountable to some recognized authority outside their own local congregation.
   - Fieldable as written; likely spread across congregational, presbyterian, episcopal, and network models.
6. A persistent pattern of manipulative or coercive leadership should normally disqualify a pastor from office.
   - Fieldable as written; strong pastoral relevance.
7. In my ministry context, the label spiritual abuse is sometimes applied to ordinary pastoral disagreement or correction.
   - Fieldable as written; tests concern about overextension of the label.
8. In my ministry context, genuinely abusive pastoral leadership is more common than churches publicly acknowledge.
   - Fieldable as written; tests concern about under-recognition.
9. Pastoral authority should include giving moral counsel about members' major life decisions outside formal church programs.
   - Fieldable as written; likely tension around pastoral scope and member autonomy.
10. Churches should publish clear procedures for bringing complaints against pastors, elders, or other leaders.
    - Fieldable as written; practical accountability item.
11. Pastors should have meaningful accountability for how they use church finances, staff authority, and confidential information.
    - Fieldable as written; concrete accountability domains.
12. Churches should distinguish between biblical submission to leaders and unquestioning compliance with leaders.
    - Fieldable as written; clarifies authority without collapsing into compliance.
