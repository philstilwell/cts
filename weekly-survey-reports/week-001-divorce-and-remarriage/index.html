Preliminary results

Week 1 Report: Divorce and Remarriage

A privacy-safe snapshot of the first revived Christian Thought Survey weekly report, based on the results visible in SurveyOL on June 15, 2026.

Preliminary public snapshot. These results should be read as current, not final, until the response window is closed and the final SurveyOL export is reviewed.

Update note: while this survey remains open, this preliminary report will be refreshed each Tuesday morning. It may also be refreshed sooner if new responses materially change the results. The final report will be marked after the response window closes and the SurveyOL export is reviewed.

Executive Summary

This first revived weekly Christian Thought Survey report is both a substantive survey on divorce and remarriage and a test of the new weekly reporting rhythm. The survey was sent through the SurveyOL Email collector to the ministry-focused CTS participant list, with responses tied privately to participant records for later aggregate analysis while public reporting remains privacy-safe.

The survey uses the CTS 0-100 credence model rather than a simple yes/no format. Respondents are asked to indicate the strength of their agreement with each statement, where 0 means strong disagreement, 50 means unsure or mixed, and 100 means strong agreement. That format is especially useful for a topic such as divorce and remarriage, where ministers often distinguish between clear biblical grounds, pastoral emergencies, contested boundary cases, and questions of church discipline.

At this preliminary snapshot, SurveyOL showed 33 complete responses from 409 invitations. These results are therefore best read as early directional signals: strong agreement is already meaningful, but divided items and subgroup patterns should be interpreted cautiously until more responses arrive and the final export is reviewed. The report will continue to be refreshed while the survey remains open.

  • The clearest agreement concerns safety and careful abuse discernment. All 33 current respondents placed the physical-safety and coercive-control items in the high-agreement band.
  • Classical grounds remain strong, but adjacent cases spread out quickly. Adultery, physical abuse, abandonment, and remarriage after a legitimate divorce show high medians; pornography, nonsexual romantic infidelity, and pastoral review show much wider distributions.
  • The most useful interpretive work will be around divided items. Formal discipline for illegitimate divorce, whether churches are too permissive, political naming, fasting expectations, and worship-source avoidance all show meaningful tension rather than a simple center of gravity.
  • The participant ballot has already produced plausible Week 2 candidates. Formal membership before public ministry, naming specific false teachers, and recreational marijuana avoidance currently lead the ranked ballot.

Strongest Patterns So Far

Broad agreement

Safety comes first

Every respondent so far agrees strongly that churches should prioritize immediate physical safety before trying to restore an abusive marriage.

Broad agreement

Abuse needs careful discernment

Respondents also strongly agree that pastors should distinguish ordinary marital conflict from patterns of coercion and control.

Most divided

Church discipline is contested

Ministers are almost evenly split on whether churches should use formal discipline when members knowingly pursue an illegitimate divorce.

Mixed views

Several practical questions remain unsettled

Responses spread out more on political warnings, fasting, worship-song sources, pornography, nonsexual infidelity, and pastoral review before remarriage.

Participant Ballot

The ballot asks participants which independent survey items they most want considered for future live survey use. SurveyOL currently shows 33 respondents answered the ballot, with 28 usable rankings per item in the score table.

Participant Suggestions

Fourteen future-question suggestions have been received so far. Raw free-text suggestions are not being published automatically; they will be reviewed, grouped, and polished for clarity, neutrality, and survey suitability before any become ballot items.

Participant suggestions are more likely to be accepted when they are already phrased as clear statements that other ministers can rate on the CTS 0-100 credence scale.

For the next participant ballot draft, five usable suggestions have been converted into rigorous 0-100 credence items. The top two Week 1 ballot items are also carried forward, producing a seven-item independent-topic ballot.
