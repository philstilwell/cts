Week 1 Report: Divorce and Remarriage

A privacy-safe snapshot of the first revived Christian Thought Survey weekly report, based on the results visible in CTS as of June 30, 2026.

Final public report. The SurveyOL Email collector is closed and this report uses the final privacy-safe aggregate summary from the reviewed CTS export.

Final close: Tuesday, June 30, 2026, 20 days after the first Week 1 production invitation window.

Finalization note: finalized June 30, 2026 from 58 complete responses after the SurveyOL Email collector was closed and the final CTS export was reviewed.

Executive Summary

This first revived weekly Christian Thought Survey report is both a substantive survey on divorce and remarriage and a test of the new weekly reporting rhythm. The survey was sent through the CTS email invitation system to the ministry-focused CTS participant list, with responses tied privately to participant records for later aggregate analysis while public reporting remains privacy-safe.

The survey uses the CTS 0-100 credence model rather than a simple yes/no format. Respondents are asked to indicate the strength of their agreement with each statement, where 0 means strong disagreement, 50 means unsure or mixed, and 100 means strong agreement. That format is especially useful for a topic such as divorce and remarriage, where ministers often distinguish between clear biblical grounds, pastoral emergencies, contested boundary cases, and questions of church discipline.

At final close, CTS showed 58 complete responses from 409 invitations. The item-level aggregates are final for the Week 1 public report; subgroup patterns remain intentionally unpublished until a separate privacy-threshold review supports them.

  • The clearest agreement concerns safety and careful abuse discernment. 57 of 58 respondents placed both the physical-safety and coercive-control items in the high-agreement band.
  • Classical grounds remain strong, but adjacent cases spread out quickly. Adultery, physical abuse, abandonment, and remarriage after a legitimate divorce show high medians; pornography, nonsexual romantic infidelity, and pastoral review show much wider distributions.
  • The most useful interpretive work will be around divided items. Formal discipline for illegitimate divorce, whether churches are too permissive, political naming, fasting expectations, and worship-source avoidance all show meaningful tension rather than a simple center of gravity.
  • The participant ballot produced the Week 2 independent items. Naming specific false teachers, formal membership before public ministry, and recreational marijuana avoidance led the ranked ballot.

The final Week 1 response is modest but useful, and the quality of the answers and suggestions already shows the promise of a larger rhythm in which participants can keep abreast of contemporary Christian doctrine and practice together.

Strongest Final Patterns

Broad agreement

Safety comes first

Almost every respondent agrees strongly that churches should prioritize immediate physical safety before trying to restore an abusive marriage.

Broad agreement

Abuse needs careful discernment

Respondents strongly agree that pastors should distinguish ordinary marital conflict from patterns of coercion and control.

Most divided

Church discipline is contested

Ministers are almost evenly split on whether churches should use formal discipline when members knowingly pursue an illegitimate divorce.

Mixed views

Several practical questions remain unsettled

Responses spread out more on political warnings, fasting, worship-song sources, pornography, nonsexual infidelity, and pastoral review before remarriage.

Participant Ballot

The ballot asks participants which independent survey items they most want considered for future live survey use. CTS currently shows 47 usable ranked ballots in the score table.

Participant Suggestions

Twenty-one future-question suggestions have been received so far. Raw free-text suggestions are not being published automatically; they will be reviewed, grouped, and polished for clarity, neutrality, and survey suitability before any become ballot items.

Participant suggestions are more likely to be accepted when they are already phrased as clear statements that other ministers can rate on the CTS 0-100 credence scale.

For the next participant ballot draft, five usable suggestions have been converted into rigorous 0-100 credence items. The top two Week 1 ballot items are also carried forward, producing a seven-item independent-topic ballot.
