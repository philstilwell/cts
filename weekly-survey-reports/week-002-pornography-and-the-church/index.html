Week 2 Complete Available-Data Report: Pornography and the Church

A privacy-safe complete available-data report for the second revived Christian Thought Survey weekly report, based on all 36 complete participant responses available before the weekly survey project closed.

Weekly survey project closure

The 2026 weekly survey project is closing.

We are deeply sorry to announce that the 2026 Christian Thought Survey weekly survey project is closing. The email platform we used experienced a bug that caused some participants to receive multiple emails, effectively spamming them. That was never intended, and we deeply apologize for the inconvenience, frustration, and loss of trust this caused.

The response from participants made clear that continuing would not be responsible. We are disappointed that we cannot carry the weekly survey project forward, and we are grateful to everyone who participated, suggested items, read the reports, or otherwise helped up to this point.

Existing public reports and archives from the weekly survey project will remain available, but no further weekly surveys, invitations, reminders, participant requests, or newsletter requests are planned.

Executive Summary

The Week 2 report now uses all 36 complete participant responses available before the weekly survey project closed. The clearest agreement concerns pastoral accountability for persistent pornography use, direct but careful leadership screening, structured pastoral care for harmed spouses, and teaching young people before pornography has already become a reported problem.

The most divided item asks whether churches should avoid presenting accountability software as a primary solution for habitual pornography use. Respondents also show meaningful spread on whether pornography is more damaging than most other hidden sins, how common pornography use among Christian women is in their ministry contexts, and whether churches are losing more ground than they are gaining.

Because the response count remains below the preferred threshold for subgroup claims, this complete available-data report should be read at the item level only. Respondent identities, respondent-level rows, and raw free-text suggestions are not published.

Strongest Patterns

Strongest agreement

Leadership accountability is high.

The pastoral-disqualification item has a mean of 78.2, with 25 of 36 responses in the high-agreement band.

Leadership screening

Direct but careful questions draw support.

The item on asking leadership candidates direct but pastorally careful questions about pornography use has a mean of 77.9.

Pastoral care

Spouses and young people stand out.

Structured care for harmed spouses and proactive youth teaching both show high-band majorities and upper quartiles at 100.

Most divided

Accountability software splits respondents.

The item on avoiding accountability software as a primary solution has the lowest mean and the most even spread across low, middle, and high bands.

Participant Ballot Results

Thirty-six participants ranked the Week 2 ballot. The top-ranked aggregate items were civil disobedience when laws violate clear biblical commands, women in church ministry roles, and IVF/assisted reproductive technologies. Baptism also received carryover-level support before the weekly survey project closed.

Participant-Nominated Draft Items

Five text-box suggestions were reviewed for accidental identifiers. None contained names, email addresses, or direct contact details. Viable suggestions were polished into credence-slider-ready statements and are preserved here as aggregate process data rather than as preparation for another survey.
