Week 2 Report: Pornography and the Church

A privacy-safe first preliminary snapshot of the second revived Christian Thought Survey weekly report, based on results visible in CTS as of June 23, 2026.

Preliminary public snapshot. These results should be read as current, not final, until the response window is closed and the final CTS export is reviewed.

Update note: last updated June 23, 2026. Current response count: 28 complete responses. While this survey remains open, this preliminary report will continue to be refreshed each Tuesday morning until final close on Tuesday, July 7, 2026. It may also be refreshed sooner if new responses materially change the results. The final report will be marked after the response window closes and the CTS export is reviewed.

Executive Summary

The first Week 2 report is preliminary and based on 28 complete responses. The clearest early agreement concerns pastoral accountability for persistent pornography use, direct but careful leadership screening, structured pastoral care for harmed spouses, and teaching young people before pornography has already become a reported problem.

The most divided early item asks whether churches should avoid presenting accountability software as a primary solution for habitual pornography use. Respondents also show meaningful spread on whether pornography is more damaging than most other hidden sins, how common pornography use among Christian women is in their ministry contexts, and whether churches are losing more ground than they are gaining.

Because the current response count is below the preferred threshold for subgroup claims, this preliminary report should be read at the item level only. Respondent identities, respondent-level rows, and raw free-text suggestions are not published.

Strongest Early Patterns

  • Leadership accountability is high. The pastoral-disqualification item currently has a mean of 83.0, with 21 of 28 responses in the high-agreement band.
  • Direct leadership screening also draws strong support. The item on asking direct but careful questions of leadership candidates has a mean of 80.5.
  • Care for harmed spouses and proactive youth teaching are clear early priorities. Both items have high-band majorities and upper quartiles at 100.
  • Accountability software is the most divided item so far. Responses split across low, middle, and high bands on whether churches should avoid presenting software as a primary solution.

All 15 Slider Items At A Glance

All 15 live items use the CTS 0-100 credence scale: 0 means strong disagreement, 50 means unsure or mixed, and 100 means strong agreement.

Participant Ballot Results

Twenty-eight participants ranked the Week 2 ballot. The top participant-vote-determined live items selected for the next survey are women in church ministry roles, civil disobedience when laws violate clear biblical commands, and IVF/assisted reproductive technologies. The third slot was tied with baptism before the Lord's Supper; IVF was selected under the tie rules because it currently has stronger immediate pastoral/ethical relevance and tension potential. Baptism remains a carryover ballot candidate.

Next Participant Ballot Draft

Five new text-box suggestions were reviewed for accidental identifiers. None contained names, email addresses, or direct contact details. The viable suggestions have been polished into credence-slider-ready statements; two non-winning Week 2 ballot items are carried over to complete the seven-item ballot.

  1. Churches should explicitly teach regular attenders that committed local church membership is a normal part of Christian discipleship.
  2. Churches should prioritize faithful Christian witness over gaining political power as their primary public posture.
  3. Churches should prioritize communities of connection and service over programs aimed mainly at individual spiritual growth.
  4. Scripture reserves the office of pastor or elder for qualified men.
  5. A complementarian church should treat a pastoral candidate's egalitarian convictions as disqualifying for its senior pastor role.
  6. Baptism should ordinarily precede participation in the Lord's Supper.
  7. Churches should teach Christians how to use artificial intelligence faithfully and critically as part of contemporary discipleship.
