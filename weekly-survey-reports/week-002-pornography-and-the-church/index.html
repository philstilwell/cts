Week 1 Encapsulation

Last week's preliminary report is now posted: Week 1, Divorce and Remarriage. As of the June 16, 2026 Tuesday refresh, the report remains at 33 complete responses. The early results show strong agreement on immediate safety in abusive marriages and on careful pastoral discernment between ordinary conflict and coercive control. Classical divorce grounds such as adultery, physical abuse, abandonment, and remarriage after a legitimate divorce currently show high agreement, while questions about pornography, nonsexual romantic infidelity, formal discipline, and pastoral review show wider spread.

Planned Live Survey Items

All 15 live items use the CTS 0-100 credence scale: 0 means strong disagreement, 50 means unsure or mixed, and 100 means strong agreement.

CTS-Administered Topic Items

  1. Pornography use is more spiritually damaging to churches today than most other hidden sins.
  2. In my ministry context, pornography use among Christian men is more common than most congregations recognize.
  3. In my ministry context, pornography use among Christian women is more common than most congregations recognize.
  4. Persistent pornography use should ordinarily disqualify a pastor from active ministry until repentance and accountability are demonstrably established.
  5. Churches should ask direct but pastorally careful questions about pornography use when evaluating candidates for leadership.
  6. Pornography use is substantially shaping sexual expectations within Christian marriages.
  7. Churches should avoid presenting accountability software as a primary solution for habitual pornography use.
  8. Pastors should address pornography from the pulpit more often than most churches currently do.
  9. Churches should treat habitual pornography use as both moral sin and a behavioral health concern.
  10. In my ministry context, churches are currently losing more ground than they are gaining against pornography.
  11. Churches should provide structured pastoral care for spouses harmed by pornography use even when the user is repentant.
  12. Churches should teach young people about pornography before parents or students report that it is already a problem.

Participant-Vote-Determined Independent Items

  1. A church should require formal membership before a person serves in any public ministry role.
  2. Pastors should normally mention specific false teachers by name when their teachings are influencing the congregation.
  3. Christians should avoid recreational marijuana use even where it is legal.

Participant Ballot For Future Items

The Week 2 survey also asks active participants to rank seven candidate items for future live survey use. Five are polished from Week 1 participant suggestions and two are Week 1 carryovers.

  1. Christians are morally obligated to disobey civil laws that require them to violate clear biblical commands.
  2. Churches should teach explicitly on the moral and pastoral implications of IVF and other assisted reproductive technologies.
  3. Women should be permitted to serve in any church ministry role for which they are spiritually gifted and otherwise qualified.
  4. Baptism should ordinarily precede participation in the Lord's Supper.
  5. Churches should teach Christians how to use artificial intelligence faithfully and critically as part of contemporary discipleship.
  6. A church should require formal membership before a person serves in any public ministry role.
  7. Pastors should normally mention specific false teachers by name when their teachings are influencing the congregation.
