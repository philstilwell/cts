Executive Summary

The Week 2 report is preliminary and now based on 36 complete responses. The clearest early agreement concerns pastoral accountability for persistent pornography use, direct but careful leadership screening, structured pastoral care for harmed spouses, and teaching young people before pornography has already become a reported problem.

The most divided early item asks whether churches should avoid presenting accountability software as a primary solution for habitual pornography use. Respondents also show meaningful spread on whether pornography is more damaging than most other hidden sins, how common pornography use among Christian women is in their ministry contexts, and whether churches are losing more ground than they are gaining.

Because the current response count is below the preferred threshold for subgroup claims, this preliminary report should be read at the item level only. Respondent identities, respondent-level rows, and raw free-text suggestions are not published.

Strongest Patterns So Far

Strongest agreement

Leadership accountability is high.

The pastoral-disqualification item currently has a mean of 78.2, with 25 of 36 responses in the high-agreement band.

Leadership screening

Direct but careful questions draw support.

The item on asking leadership candidates direct but pastorally careful questions about pornography use currently has a mean of 77.9.

Pastoral care

Spouses and young people stand out.

Structured care for harmed spouses and proactive youth teaching both show high-band majorities and upper quartiles at 100.

Most divided

Accountability software splits respondents.

The item on avoiding accountability software as a primary solution has the lowest mean and the most even spread across low, middle, and high bands.
