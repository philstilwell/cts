# CTS Topic Bank Tension Review

Initial review date: June 2, 2026

This document reviews `CTS 2026 Weekly Topic Bank.md` for whether the 50 weekly topics and their 12 items are likely to produce meaningful distributional data rather than near-unanimous agreement. The goal is not to make every item provocative. The goal is to keep each item semantically clear while making sure the proposition contains a real doctrinal, pastoral, moral, practical, or predictive tradeoff.

The topic bank is structurally sound: 50 topics, each with 12 items.

## Working Standard

A strong CTS item should satisfy four conditions:

1. Clear proposition: participants can tell exactly what they are rating without extra context.
2. Credence-slider fit: 0, 50, and 100 all make sense as possible responses.
3. Tension potential: faithful ministers could plausibly disagree, or the item could reveal a wide spread of confidence.
4. Current relevance: the item connects to live doctrinal, pastoral, practical, or moral pressures in today's Christian world.

## How To Increase Tension Without Losing Clarity

Use these patterns when an item feels too obvious:

- Add a cost or constraint: "even when...", "before...", "unless...", "despite..."
- Turn a broad good into a policy threshold: "should require", "should normally refuse", "should publicly name", "should separate from..."
- Make the item comparative: "more important than", "more harmful than", "more likely than..."
- Add a prediction: "will increase", "will become", "will substantially..."
- Ask about a boundary: "should disqualify", "should be required", "should be treated as first-order", "should prevent cooperation..."
- Ask about actual ministry context: "In my ministry context..."

Avoid relying too often on items that say only "churches should do more", "pastors should be careful", "leaders should be transparent", or "people should be treated with compassion." Those are usually true but may not generate useful disagreement unless a tradeoff is named.

## Week 1 Independent Item Standard

The 3 independent live items and 7 participant-nominated ballot items should be:

- Orthogonal to the weekly CTS-administered topic.
- Not copied from the current 50-topic bank.
- Clear enough to answer immediately.
- Likely to produce meaningful tension or spread.
- Relevant to current Christian doctrine, practice, and moral judgment.

The older 200-item CTS survey may be indexed as a reference pool for seed ideas, but current weekly independent and ballot items should be rewritten for present clarity and should not be copied mechanically.

## Example Revisions

These are examples of the kind of sharpening that can preserve clarity while increasing tension:

- Consensus-prone: "Pastors should know the limits of their competence when caring for people at risk of self-harm."
- More tension-rich: "Pastors should be expected to refer people at risk of self-harm to licensed mental-health professionals even when the person prefers pastoral care only."

- Consensus-prone: "Churches should train members to explain the gospel without relying on insider Christian vocabulary."
- More tension-rich: "Churches should revise evangelism training more around post-Christian assumptions than around traditional gospel-presentation methods."

- Consensus-prone: "Pastors are often lonely because they lack safe friendships."
- More tension-rich: "Pastors should maintain close friendships outside their congregation even when those friendships reduce their local availability."

- Consensus-prone: "Churches should provide more practical support for single adults."
- More tension-rich: "Churches should avoid organizing most adult discipleship around marital or parenting status."

- Consensus-prone: "Churches should train members in digital discipleship."
- More tension-rich: "Churches should treat social-media habits as a normal subject of pastoral discipleship and accountability."

## Recommendation

Before each weekly survey is finalized, review that week's 12 CTS-administered items and ask:

- Which 3 items are likely consensus anchors?
- Which 6-8 items are likely to produce meaningful spread?
- Which 2-3 items are likely to reveal key tensions worth reporting?
- Does any item sound morally obvious without naming a real cost or boundary?
- Does any item need simpler wording before it becomes usable as a 0-100 credence item?

The ideal weekly survey should include a few consensus anchors for context, but most items should contain enough tension to generate interpretable distributions and useful "Key tensions" reporting.
